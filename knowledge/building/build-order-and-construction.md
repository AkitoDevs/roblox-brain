# Build order and construction logic for environments

Each step below earned its place because a real build visibly improved when it was followed, or failed in a diagnosed way when it was skipped.

**0. Say the limits first.** What you can't judge (motion, feel in Play), which steps need a person (imports, uploads, approval), and the ceiling of the chosen tool (Parts can't carve; Studio curves come out as segments).

**1. Brief: what makes it look the way it does.** Before the first part: style language and palette, scale against a 5-stud stand-in, the three largest shapes, the structure story, material recipes, the key light, the focal point and leading line. With a reference, read these from the reference, not just its layout. Without a brief, palette and exaggeration drift.

**2. Cameras before detail.** With a reference, solve the camera from vanishing lines and put landmarks in the foreground as well as the far field; sanity-check one real object size (a door, a step, a sofa). An assumed eye height once produced a five-metre sofa. Without a reference, fix a capture set now (front, side, back, elevated, player height, stand-in in frame) and use it for every later check. Rough final lighting goes in now too, before any detail.

**3. Big shapes, then crop by crop.** Block out primary volumes and check them through the camera. Then cut the reference into part crops (one railing run, a window bay, a stair flight) and for each: model it, compare reference, before and after at the same crop, and note in words what matches. Fill what the reference can't show with educated guesses from how such things are normally built.

**4. Construction logic, including hidden parts.** Build everything the way it would really stand:
- Roofs have overhang, fascia, ridge and tile rows.
- Walls have a base, body and top with real thickness.
- Openings are recessed with a frame, lintel and sill, never stuck on; a close-up should show reveal shadow.
- Loads reach the ground: no floating parapets or slabs.
- Stairs go in before the floor.
- Curved Part walls use mitre wedges; arches get springer wedges, for zero-gap joints.
- Every surface junction gets a line or value change (baseboard, corner post, trim).
- Close rooms and build the hidden side: front-only captures hide leaks, such as a backdrop visible through an open alcove.
- Spread detail along the player route, not only on the hero face.

Fixing the look with construction (a roof system, wall base and top, recessed openings, a plinth into the ground) turns a textured box into a believable building far more than surface patterns do.
