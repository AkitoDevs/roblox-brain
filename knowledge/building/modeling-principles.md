# Modeling props and buildings that read well

1. **Silhouette first.** A model must read as a solid shape from far away. Exaggerate the defining feature (a chunky top, a tapered base, a big canopy). Squint at a screenshot to check.
2. **Shape language carries mood.** Round and soft for friendly areas, boxy and sturdy for utility, sharp and angular for danger. One language per area.
3. **Big, medium, small.** Large primary forms; medium secondary forms (bands, trims, panels, caps); small details (bolts, studs, pebbles) concentrated at focal points. Leave rest areas plain.
4. **No raw boxes.** Add a slightly larger cap on top, a plinth at the base, trim strips, inset or raised panels, wedge chamfers. Layered plates read as bevels.
5. **Colour blocking and value contrast.** Main, secondary and small accent colour per object; neighbouring pieces differ in value so forms separate. Use two or three shades of one hue on big surfaces instead of one flat colour.
6. **Variation kills repetition.** Jitter size, a few degrees of yaw and 5 to 10% shade on repeated items; group in odd numbers; avoid perfect grids unless the design is formal on purpose.
7. **Terraces and layers.** Steps, ledges, overhangs and stacked plates create shadow lines; even a flat plaza gains depth from a curb or a raised dais.
8. **Story props.** Small arrangements that imply life: a campfire with a pot, crates mid-unpacking, a weapon rack with one sword missing.
9. **Material honesty.** Wood has planks, grain and metal bands; metal has seams and rivets; stone has chunky blocks and chipped corners; fabric sags.
10. **Emissive sparingly.** Glow belongs on focal accents only. To calm an over-blooming neon strip, darken its colour instead of adding transparency.
11. **Defined edges.** Dark trim or a darker shade on outer edges gives a cartoon outline without Highlight objects.
12. **Break big flat surfaces** with panel lines, bands, vents, decals or patterns so their scale reads.
13. **Consistent scale.** Measure against a 5-stud character: doors 7 to 8 studs, counters about 3.2, steps 0.6 to 1, railings about 3.
14. **Modular kits.** Build reusable modules (lamp, crate, barrel, planter, fence, rock cluster) in one style and assemble scenes from them.
15. **Designed placeholders.** A spot waiting for content gets an intentional stand-in (a veiled statue, an empty plinth with a plaque, a sealed door), never a blank block.
