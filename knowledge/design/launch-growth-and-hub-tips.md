# Launch, growth and hub design tips

Every line here is labelled **Unverified tip:** because it comes from outside reading and has not been checked against Roblox documentation or tested by us. Re-check platform rules before relying on them.

**Growth**
- **Unverified tip:** plan the reason for a player to return on day 8 (an update, a new feature or a live event) before release, and lay out a 30-day calendar with updates in weeks 2 to 4. Recommendation systems may weigh the weeks after launch more than the first day, and a slow steady climb tends to beat one-off spikes.
- **Unverified tip:** for gameplay videos on the experience page, show real current gameplay only: no cinematic trailer, no voice-over, nothing better than what players get. Claims of large recommendation gains from this are anecdotal.
- **Unverified tip:** an engagement-goal ad campaign may keep running after you hit your target; pause it by hand once the audience you wanted is reached.
- **Unverified tip:** published industry medians for retention are far lower than the "good" numbers some vendors quote (day-1 in the single digits to low teens, day-7 around one or two percent). Don't use vendor targets as a pass/fail gate for a small game.
- **Unverified tip:** call a game "sustained" only if it keeps getting updates and its player count after 30 days is measured, not guessed, rather than going by a recent update date.

**Design**
- **Unverified tip:** three levers make a shared game interesting: scarcity of a shared resource, an advantage for moving first, and deliberate imperfection (a rule players can bend or exploit playfully).
- **Unverified tip:** in a minigame hub, a global rule that rotates every few minutes (low gravity, no jumping, reversed controls) gives every round a different feel for little extra content.
