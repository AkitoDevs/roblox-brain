# Trees that don't look like a ball on a stick

**Anatomy rules for every tree**
1. **The trunk is a cone,** wider at the base, with a visible root flare at the soil line. Palms are the exception: a roughly constant ringed column with fronds from one point.
2. **Forks are Y or V shapes, never plus signs.** Children are thinner than the parent and together roughly as thick.
3. **Branches leave with a small swelling** that starts inside the trunk, with no gap.
4. **Hierarchy:** trunk, limbs, branches, twigs, clumps, with at least three visible levels. Lower limbs are longer; the top is shorter.
5. **The crown is made of clumps on branch ends:** 3 to 7 distinct clumps for a stylised tree, different sizes, overlapping unevenly, with notched outlines and gaps for sky.
6. **Light:** lit top, dark underside, darkest near the trunk, one direction.
7. **Asymmetry:** a lean, uneven limbs, no mirror twins.
8. **Keep limbs visible.** Leaves sit on the outer part of each limb and the inner limb is bare. Spreading trees have a short thick trunk (about a sixth to a third of the height) and a scalloped crown outline with a dark underside band.
9. **Pick the envelope first:** oval, wide dome, vase, umbrella or flat top, cone, column, or column plus burst (palms). Place clumps inside it.
10. **The skeleton is a fan:** the trunk divides into 2 to 4 limbs by about 25 to 40% of the height, with 3 to 4 fork levels, limbs leaving at about 30 to 60 degrees from vertical and curving gently while thinning. Lean the trunk and put the crown centre off to one side.
11. **A tuft at every branch tip.** Umbrella trees carry flat-bottomed pads on a bare, often leaning trunk. A "lollipop" is only wrong when the pole is straight and unforked, the top is a smooth sphere, and there are no gaps or dark underside.
12. **Choose the trunk-to-crown ratio on purpose.** Crown width is roughly 0.7 to 1.5 times the height for spreading trees and 0.3 to 0.5 for oval, column and conifer shapes.
